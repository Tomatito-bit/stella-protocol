# Gravity, Radiation and Distance (team research, November 14, 2026)

## Hazard: gravity fields
- Floating without gravity causes motion sickness, bone and muscle loss, and vision problems.
- After landing on the Moon or Mars, astronauts need time to adapt before doing critical tasks.
- **A Mars mission involves 3 different gravity fields.** The transition affects:
  - spatial orientation
  - eye-hand coordination
  - balance
  - walking

  Some crew members get space motion sickness.
- **Artificial-gravity rings:** they use centrifugal force. Moving from the zero-g core to the ring means dealing with the Coriolis effect.
- **Asteroid belt:** microgravity (~0.001 g). Moving around takes harpoons, gas thrusters (MMU) and magnetic anchors.
- **Jupiter and its moons:**
  - The planet has no solid surface.
  - Gravity assists put high loads on the spacecraft.
  - The radiation belts damage electronics.

## Hazard: space radiation
- **Three threats:**
  - **Galactic cosmic rays (GCR):** constant, impossible to block 100%.
  - **Solar particle events (SPE):** sudden and dangerous within hours.
  - **Planetary radiation belts:** Jupiter, Juno.
- **ALARA principle:** As Low As Reasonably Achievable.
- **A. Shielding:**
  - Lead doesn't work well because it creates secondary particles. NASA uses **polyethylene**, which is rich in hydrogen.
  - **Water and propellant protect.** Orion surrounds the cabin with tanks and supplies.
  - **Titanium vault** for the computers (Juno).
- **B. Operations:**
  - **Storm shelter:** the crew moves to the center of the spacecraft.
  - **Safe mode:** on robotic spacecraft.
  - **Elliptical orbits:** dive in fast, take data and leave the radiation zone.
- **C. Medicine:**
  - Real-time dosimeters and a **career dose limit**: when an astronaut gets close, their flying career ends.
  - Space-weather satellites give warnings minutes to hours ahead.

## Hazard: distance from Earth
- **Radiation by destination:**
  - Moon: short missions, days to weeks.
  - Mars: 6 to 9 months each way, with a large dose.
  - Jupiter: extreme radiation.
- **Signal delay:**
  - Moon: seconds.
  - Mars: 4 to 24 min one way.
  - Jupiter: 33 to 54 min.

  The crew must be autonomous.
- Long confinement: fatigue, insomnia, disrupted circadian rhythm and friction.
- **Solutions:**
  - shielded shelters
  - active magnetic shields (superconducting solenoids), still experimental
  - daily resistance exercise
  - radioprotective drugs and antioxidants
  - psychological screening
  - light therapy

## Team ideas for the game
**Design:**
- Shielding level: standard, polyethylene or titanium vault. More shielding costs more and weighs more.
- Full tanks around the cabin protect the crew.

**Operations, solar storm or radiation belt event (3 options):**
- **A) Shelter / safe mode:** no science that turn, almost no damage.
- **B) Change orbit:** spends propellant and you keep operating.
- **C) Take the risk:** crew health drops and electronics degrade.

**Interface:**
- **Biometric HUD** instead of a health bar: heart rate, blood pressure and fluids shifting to the head.
- **Gravity transition:** the camera tilts, ears ring and vision clears gradually.
- **Space motion sickness:** controls feel "floaty" for a moment.
- **Physics per world:** falling and braking differ on the Moon, on Mars and in microgravity.
