# NASA Mission Model (team research)

Source: the team's document "NASA Mission Model" (November 14, 2026).

## Project phases
- **Phase A · Concept formulation:** science goal, feasibility and initial cost. A mission to the Moon doesn't cost the same as one to Jupiter.
- **Phase B · Preliminary design:** spacecraft architecture, propulsion, tanks, power and risk mitigation.
- **Phase C · Final design and fabrication:** building parts, thermal tests and validation.
- **Phase D · Assembly and launch:** integration, ground tests and liftoff.
- **Phase E · Operations:** monitoring, managing crew or rover resources, trajectory corrections and sending science data.

## Safety measures
- **Redundancy:** critical systems have backups to avoid total loss.
- **Radiation and space weather:** shielding, and **safe mode** during solar storms or critical radiation.
- **Critical consumables:** oxygen, water, power and food are tracked, with margins in case a maneuver is delayed.

## Preparation
- **Crisis simulations:** Mission Control rehearses hundreds of simulated failures.
- **Environmental testing:** vacuum chamber, vibration (simulates launch) and extreme temperatures.

## How it enters the game
| From the document | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| Phases A to E | Screens follow the phases: Concept → Design → Testing → Launch → Operations |
| Redundancy | Each critical system can carry a backup: it costs mass and money but prevents losing the mission |
| Safe mode | A daily option and an automatic response to storms or radiation |
| Consumables | Oxygen, water, food and power bars, with margins |
| Environmental testing | Before launch you can pay for vacuum, vibration and thermal tests that reveal hidden defects |
| Simulations | Training the crew raises their success rate in emergencies |
