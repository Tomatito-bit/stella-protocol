# Risks to the Crew

NASA's Human Research Program groups everything into **5 hazards**, which are linked to **more than 30 documented health risks**. The highest-priority ("red") risks for Mars are:
- radiation
- Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS)
- behavioral health
- food and nutrition

## The 5 hazards and what they cause

### 1. Space radiation
- **What it is:** cosmic rays and solar storms, once outside Earth's magnetic field.
- **Effects:**
  - higher **cancer** risk
  - **central nervous system** damage: memory and attention
  - **cardiovascular** disease
  - cataracts
  - in a strong solar storm without shelter: **acute radiation syndrome**, with nausea and vomiting
- **Protection:** shelters with water or polyethylene, solar storm alerts and career dose limits.

### 2. Isolation and confinement
- **Effects:**
  - stress, anxiety and depression
  - fatigue
  - team **conflicts**
  - lower performance
- **Data point:** in the HERA analog, crews isolated for weeks gradually lose their ability to solve problems together.
- **Sleep:** astronauts often sleep less than 6 hours.
- **Protection:** psychological screening, private calls with family, schedules and exercise.

### 3. Distance from Earth
- **No quick return.** Coming back from Mars takes months.
- **Communication delay** of up to ~22 min one way with Mars. In emergencies, **the crew decides alone**.
- **Limited medical care:** little diagnostic equipment, and medicines degrade over time.
- **Real example (January 2026):** NASA carried out the **first medical evacuation** in the history of the International Space Station.
  - Crew-11 came home more than a month early because an astronaut (Mike Fincke, as he later said) needed tests only available on Earth.
  - It was not an emergency, but the station lacks full diagnostic equipment.

### 4. Gravity (or the lack of it)
- **Bones:** about 1 to 1.5% of bone mass is lost per month without exercise. This raises the risk of **fractures** and **kidney stones**.
- **Muscles:** atrophy, especially in the legs and back. Astronauts exercise about 2 h per day on the station.
- **Heart and circulation:** on return, dizziness and fainting when standing up (orthostatic intolerance).
- **SANS:** fluids shift toward the head, the eye changes shape and vision blurs.
- **Space motion sickness:** nausea and disorientation in the first days.
- **Balance and coordination:** change when moving between gravity fields (for example, landing on Mars).
- **Blood clots:** in 2020 a clot was found in an astronaut's jugular vein on the ISS. It was treated on board with blood thinners, with doctors guiding from the ground.

### 5. Hostile, closed environment
- **Air:** high **CO₂** causes headaches and slow thinking.
  - On Apollo 13 the crew improvised filters with tape, plastic and cardboard.
- **Immune system:** it weakens and "dormant" viruses (such as herpes) reactivate. Allergies and infections become more common.
- **Lunar dust:**
  - It is sharp and electrically charged.
  - Apollo 17 astronaut Harrison Schmitt had "**lunar hay fever**": watery eyes, sore throat and sneezing.
  - In the lab, lunar dust simulants killed up to 90% of lung cells.
- **Noise:** constant fan noise can damage hearing.
- **Spacewalk injuries:**
  - Fingernails detaching because of the gloves.
  - Shoulder injuries.
  - In 2013 astronaut Luca Parmitano's helmet filled with water during a spacewalk.
- **Decompression:** decompression sickness when changing pressure to go outside.
- **Food:** vitamins degrade; repetitive food kills appetite and intake drops.
- **Fire and leaks:**
  - Mir (1997): a fire on board.
  - Soyuz 11 (1971): the capsule lost pressure on return and its 3 crew members died.
  - Apollo 1 (1967): a fire on the ground killed 3 astronauts.

## Summary for the game
| Hazard | Bar or event | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Radiation | Low health, solar storm event | Shielding, shelter, early warning |
| Isolation | Low morale, fights | Rest, talking, a mediating commander |
| Distance | Emergencies without Houston | Trained, rested crew |
| Gravity | Health drops over time | Daily exercise (costs science time) |
| Closed environment | CO₂, illness, fire | Repairs, filters, a doctor on board |
