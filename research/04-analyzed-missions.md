# Analyzed Missions

## Current

### James Webb Space Telescope
- **What it does:** observes the first galaxies of the universe and exoplanet atmospheres in infrared.
- **Launch and location:** launched December 25, 2021. It sits at the L2 point, 1.5 million km from Earth.
- **Risks:** 344 single-point failures, mostly in the sunshield and mirror deployment. It is too far away to be repaired.
- **What happened:** every deployment worked. In 2022 a micrometeoroid hit a mirror segment.
- **Game idea:** a risky deployment phase, and deciding how much to test before launch.

### Hubble Space Telescope
- **What it does:** has been in low Earth orbit since 1990, taking high-resolution images of deep space.
- **Problems:**
  - Its mirror was ground incorrectly and was fixed in 1993 by astronauts.
  - Its gyroscopes wear out; since 2024 it has operated with only one.
- **What makes it special:** it is the only telescope repaired by spacewalking astronauts (5 servicing missions).
- **Game idea:** in-orbit repairs, aging parts and safe mode.

### Perseverance Rover (Mars)
- **What it does:** explores Jezero Crater, an ancient lake, looking for signs of ancient microbial life, and caches rock samples.
- **Risks:**
  - The landing: the "7 minutes of terror".
  - Dust.
  - Rocks that crumble.
  - Pebbles jamming the sample mechanism.
- **Status in 2026:** the program to bring the samples to Earth lost support in its original form.
- **Game idea:** choosing where to explore and which samples to keep with limited space.

### Chandra X-ray Observatory
- **What it does:** has operated since 1999, imaging high-energy phenomena such as black holes and supernova remnants in X-rays.
- **Risks:**
  - Aging: its thermal insulation degrades and the spacecraft runs hotter.
  - Budget-cut threats.
- **Game idea:** missions that must keep delivering science so they are not cancelled.

### Parker Solar Probe
- **What it does:** flies through the Sun's corona. It is the fastest human-made object, at ~690,000 km/h at its closest approach.
- **Record:** in December 2024 it passed ~6.1 million km from the Sun, and it has repeated that distance on later passes.
- **Risks:** extreme heat, high-speed dust and radiation.
- **Protection:** an 11.4 cm carbon heat shield.
- **Game idea:** keep the spacecraft oriented with the shield facing the Sun, or it is lost.

## Upcoming

### Artemis Program
- **What it is:** humans returning to the Moon, at the lunar South Pole, where there is ice in permanently shadowed craters. It lays the groundwork for Mars.
- **What has flown:**
  - **Artemis I (2022):** uncrewed flight. The heat shield lost material.
  - **Artemis II (April 2026):** crewed flight around the Moon, with a modified reentry. The heat shield performed much better.
- **Risks:**
  - The heat shield.
  - Lunar dust, which is toxic and abrasive.
  - Extreme cold in the shadows.
  - Radiation.
  - Landing on uneven terrain.
- **Game idea:** the flagship crewed mission, with picking a landing site, caring for the crew and dealing with dust.

### Dragonfly (Titan, a moon of Saturn)
- **What it is:** an 8-rotor drone powered by a nuclear generator (MMRTG) that will fly between sites on Titan to study organic chemistry.
- **Dates:** launch planned for 2028, arriving in the mid-2030s.
- **Why it can fly there:** Titan has an atmosphere denser than Earth's and low gravity.
- **Risks:**
  - Cold of −179 °C.
  - It flies on its own, because the communication delay is over an hour.
  - Landings on dunes.
- **Game idea:** planning short flights with limited energy.

### Europa Clipper
- **What it is:** studies whether Jupiter's moon Europa, with an ocean under its ice, could host life.
- **Dates:** launched October 2024; arrives at Jupiter in 2030.
- **Risks:** Jupiter's radiation, which forces quick flybys of Europa instead of orbiting it, and transistors less resistant than expected.
- **Game idea:** balancing science against accumulated radiation dose.

### Astronaut selection
- NASA selects and trains new crews for the Moon and Mars.
- **Game idea:** building the crew by choosing profiles (pilot, doctor, engineer, scientist), each with strengths and risks.
